Who Am I, Really?

💪
Physical Self

I take my physical health seriously — it is my temple. My daily routine includes workouts: lifting, bodyweight training, and the sports I love. Volleyball, basketball, and billiards are not just games for me — they sharpen my reflexes, build discipline, and keep my body strong. I believe that a healthy body fuels a sharp mind, and I carry myself with confidence that comes from consistent effort.

Body is a Temple

Spiritual Self

My faith is the cornerstone of who I am. I am deeply religious — prayer and Scripture are daily anchors that keep me grounded, humble, and hopeful. I believe that everything I pursue — my studies, my sport, my future career — is done for a greater purpose. My values of integrity, compassion, and service are rooted in my relationship with God. When challenges arise, faith reminds me that I am never alone.

Rooted in Faith

Material Self

I live simply but purposefully. My lifestyle reflects a balance between practical needs and meaningful experiences. I value quality over quantity — whether it is equipment for sports, food for my healthy diet, or tools for my studies. I am working toward financial independence as an IT professional, where I can provide a stable life for my family without compromising my values or my well-being.

I express love through acts of service and quality time. I am charming and warm in relationships — I believe in showing genuine care, listening deeply, and being present. I approach romantic relationships with the same intentionality as my faith: with respect, purity, and long-term commitment in mind. For me, love is a choice and a responsibility, not just a feeling.

Digital Self

Online, I present myself as a responsible, positive presence. I am mindful of what I post — it should reflect my values and not contradict who I am offline. My digital identity as a future IT professional is being built intentionally: I contribute to tech discussions, showcase my learning, and avoid online behaviors that could compromise my integrity. The offline me and the online me are one and the same — consistent, grounded, and genuine.

How Technology Shapes My Identity

Technology has given me access to learning, community, and self-expression I otherwise would not have. It has shaped how I study, how I stay connected with my faith community, and how I train. I see it as a tool — and like all tools, its value depends entirely on the hands that wield it.

Ethical Responsibilities in the Digital World

01 Protect user privacy and data — treat every user's information as sacred trust, not a resource to exploit.
02 Build accessible technology — good software serves everyone, not just those who are already privileged.
03 Refuse to participate in the spread of misinformation — as a digital creator, my output carries weight.
04 Uphold professional integrity — never cut corners in code or in character, because quality and honesty go hand in hand.

Letter to Myself

A Note to My Present Self

Written with intention · 2025
Dear Present Me,

I know this season feels heavy sometimes. The coursework piles up, the expectations are loud, and there are days when the gap between who you are and who you want to become feels impossibly wide. But I need you to hear this: you are not behind. You are exactly where you need to be.

You have something powerful that many people search their whole lives for — faith. It is not just a label or a Sunday ritual. It is the quiet voice that tells you to get up after the hard days, to play one more set, to study one more hour. Let it lead you. Let it steady you when the pressure mounts and the answers are unclear.

Your love for sport is not a distraction — it is who you are. The volleyball court has taught you to trust your teammates. The basketball game has taught you to read the moment and respond. The billiards table has taught you patience, angles, and the long game. These lessons belong in your career too. IT, like sport, is about strategy, collaboration, and performing under pressure.

Take care of your body. Not because you are obsessed with it — but because it is the only one you have, and the best version of you requires it to be strong. Eat well. Rest deeply. Move every day. The discipline you build in the gym will carry you through the hardest debugging sessions and the most stressful deadlines.

And remember this above all: charm without character is empty. You have natural warmth and charisma — use them to serve, to encourage, to include. Be the person in the room who makes everyone feel seen. That is the kind of leader the world needs, in tech and everywhere else.

Version 2.0 is not a destination — it is a direction. Keep walking.

With love, faith, and full confidence in you,

Your Future Self
